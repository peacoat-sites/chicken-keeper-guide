Florida [backyard flock owners](/what-the-cdc-h5n1-survey-revealed-about-backyard-flock-owners/) inside Okeechobee and western St. Lucie counties woke up on November 20 to news that changed what they can legally do with their birds. Cal-Maine Foods, the country's biggest egg producer, confirmed HPAI at its Okeechobee County facility on November 18, 2026. The result: approximately 1.4 million laying hens and 360,000 pullets culled, and a state quarantine order in effect that requires written permission from Florida officials before you can move even one backyard chicken out of the zone. That's not a recommendation. It's a legal requirement, and violating it carries real consequences.

This isn't a scare story about commercial facilities that don't apply to small flocks. The national picture makes that clear. According to APHIS data for the 30 days ending November 19, 2026, there were 38 confirmed HPAI flocks across the country. Twenty-three of them were backyard flocks. In October 2026 alone, through just the 24th of that month, USDA confirmed 14 backyard flock infections, double September's count of seven, a sharp acceleration tied directly to fall wild bird migration. California has seen six backyard flock detections since October 5, 2026, compared to only one commercial flock detection in the same window.

## If You're Inside the Florida Quarantine Zone, Here's What That Means Practically

The quarantine stretches through Okeechobee County and into the western portion of St. Lucie County. If your property falls inside it, you cannot legally sell birds, give birds away, transport them to a show, move them to a friend's property, or relocate them even temporarily without written authorization from the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. Contact FDACS through the official Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services website or your county extension office to reach the appropriate agricultural emergency line. That line handles agricultural emergencies, and this qualifies.

Do not wait to see if your birds look sick. HPAI moves fast and birds can shed virus before showing symptoms. Report any sudden unexplained deaths, neurological symptoms, or a drop in egg production immediately. FDACS and your county extension office can arrange testing. The state covers depopulation and disposal costs if infection is confirmed in a registered flock, which is another reason to make sure your flock is registered even before an outbreak.

If you're outside the quarantine zone, don't assume you're safe from restrictions. Florida can expand quarantine boundaries if new detections occur. Keep track of FDACS updates directly rather than relying on news aggregators.

## Biosecurity Measures That Actually Work Right Now

Wild waterfowl, the primary vector for HPAI spread during migration, are moving through Florida in large numbers this month. The practical response isn't complicated, but it requires consistency.

Cover your run. A fully enclosed run with hardware cloth on top prevents direct contact with wild birds and limits exposure to droppings. Temporary netting works if permanent covering isn't in place. Don't underestimate open-top runs, they're the single most common exposure point in backyard HPAI cases.

Change your clothes and footwear before entering the coop and after leaving. Keep a dedicated pair of boots at the coop door. If you've visited a feed store, a farm, or any location with other poultry, treat yourself as a potential vector before going near your birds.

Water sources matter more than most keepers think. Shared water between wild birds and chickens, even a puddle near the run, is a documented transmission route. Move water containers inside covered areas. Remove anything that attracts standing water near the coop.

Stop flock additions for now. This is a terrible time to bring in new birds from auctions, swaps, or hatcheries unless you can quarantine them completely for at least 30 days in a separate airspace. The Coop Size Calc can help you figure out whether a true quarantine setup is physically feasible on your property before you buy birds.

## Understanding the Risk, Without Exaggerating It

The CDC has reported 75 human H5 bird flu cases and three deaths in the United States since February 2024, the vast majority among farm and poultry workers with direct, repeated exposure to infected birds. The risk to the general public remains low by current CDC assessment. That said, don't handle sick or dead birds without gloves and an N95. Wash hands thoroughly after any coop work. These are basic precautions that apply every flu season, not just during outbreaks.

Your eggs are safe to eat if cooked to 160°F, the USDA-recommended minimum internal temperature for egg dishes. There's no documented case of HPAI transmission through properly cooked eggs or poultry meat.

## The Autumn Acceleration Is Real and Not Over

The doubling of backyard HPAI detections from September to October 2026 isn't random. Fall migration brings infected wild birds into contact with backyard flocks across a much wider geography than summer patterns allow. California's six backyard detections since early October, most recently in San Benito County on November 3, show this isn't a Florida-specific problem.

Here's a comparison of the recent HPAI backyard vs. commercial flock breakdown to help put scope in context:

| Time Period | Backyard Flocks Confirmed | Commercial Flocks Confirmed | Total Birds Affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 2026 | 7 | Mixed | Not specified |
| October 1-24, 2026 | 14 | Mixed | Not specified |
| 30 days ending Nov 19, 2026 | 23 | 15 | 3.94 million |
| Cal-Maine Okeechobee alone | 0 (commercial) | 1 | ~1.76 million |

The November surge in total bird numbers is almost entirely driven by that single Cal-Maine commercial facility. But the backyard flock count rising to 23 out of 38 nationally shows that small operations are bearing a proportionally heavy share of detections.

Migration doesn't end in November. Keep biosecurity protocols running through February at minimum. Wild bird activity in Florida runs through late winter, and the state's climate means resident waterfowl are present year-round.

The Florida quarantine is a real legal constraint right now, not a maybe. If you're in the zone, make one call to FDACS today before you do anything with your birds. If you're outside it, treat the autumn spike as the warning it is and button up your biosecurity now, because this wave isn't done moving through.
